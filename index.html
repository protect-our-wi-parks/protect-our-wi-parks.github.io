    ℹ️ About this app
    

      
    What it does
    
      
    It shows you Wisconsin's most common invasive plants (plants from other places that crowd out
        Wisconsin's own plants) and helps you report one to the Wisconsin DNR when you see it.
    

      
    How to use it
    
      
      
        
    • You know the plant: tap it on the front page, then I found this: report it. Show where it is, then report it.
      • 
        
    • You're not sure: tap Not sure? Take a photo. The Pl@ntNet AI guesses the plant,
          we check Wisconsin's invasive list, then you can report it.
      • 
        
    • Reporting: we show the plant and the exact spot so you can copy them, then open the DNR's own report page.
      • 
      
    

      
    The AI can be wrong
    
      
    That's why we always say "possible" and show how sure the AI is.
        Experts at the DNR make the final call. If you know the plant for sure, pick it from the list instead.
    

      
    Stay safe
    
      
    Don't touch plants you don't know. Wild parsnip and giant hogweed can burn skin, and poison hemlock is very
        poisonous. Explore with an adult and stay on public land.
    

      
    Where your photo and location go
    
      
      
        
    • If you use the plant AI, your photo is made smaller and sent through our plant helper to Pl@ntNet
          to be identified. Nothing is saved, and there's no login.
      • 
        
    • Your location stays on your phone. We show it to you at the end so you can copy it into your DNR report.
      • 
        
    • The map pictures come from OpenStreetMap, so they can tell roughly which area you're looking at.
      • 
      
    

      
    Reporting to the DNR
    
      
    This app never sends anything by itself. You copy the details and report through the DNR's own page:
        DNR: Report invasive species
